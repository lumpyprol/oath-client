# Rulings

Rulebook edition (Q5): the **Buried Giant Studios rules library, Oath,
printing p1** — <https://rules.buriedgiant.com/?product=oath&locale=en-US&printing=p1>
— which reproduces the *Law of Oath* (October 20, 2020, Leder Games) with
identical section numbering. Code citations use `Law §x.y`; setup steps are
`Law §1.n` (step *n* of the Setup chapter).

Open point: the P1 card data was reconciled against a 2nd-printing-or-later
card set, while this reference is printing p1. If a card-text ruling ever
differs by printing, record it below.

Anything the rulebook leaves ambiguous, and any table ruling the group makes,
is recorded here with the date and the rulebook edition it interprets.

| 2026-09-11 | Does a player TIED for a "most" goal — most sites (Supremacy / Vision of Conquest), most relics and banners (Protection / Vision of Sanctuary) — *meet* that goal? It decides both who holds the Oathkeeper title and whether a tied Exile wins a Visionary Win (§3.2), and neither section says. | **Yes, a tie meets the goal.** The evidence is §2.11's own tie rule: "If the title's holder becomes tied with another player **for meeting the Oathkeeper goal**, the holder keeps the token." That sentence only parses if both tied players meet it — under a strict reading ("more than everyone") a tie would mean NOBODY meets the goal and the rule would be dead text. So `seatsMeetingOath` returns every seat at the maximum, and the tie rules (holder keeps it; holder chooses among others) do the rest. The same predicate decides a Visionary Win, so a tied Exile with the Vision of Conquest wins on their Wake. Recorded because it changes who wins a game, not just who holds a token. | Law §2.11, §3.2, §3.4.3 |
| 2026-09-12 | Law §4.3.2 says "Move any secret tokens on denizens, edifices, and relics to **your** board." Read literally, "any" would sweep secrets off ANOTHER player's advisers into the resting player's pool. | **Scoped to cards the resting player could have paid onto**: their own advisers, plus every card in play at a site. A secret only ever arrives on a card as a power cost (§7.1.2), and §7.1.1 puts another player's advisers out of reach — so one can never be yours to take back, and sweeping it would be a steal rather than a return. The contrast with §4.3.1 is the tell: that one IS global precisely because favor goes to the **banks**, belonging to nobody. Site cards are swept globally and that is safe rather than generous — costs are paid on your own turn at your own site (§5.2.1, §5.3.2) and every turn ends in a Rest, so a secret on a site card is one this seat placed this turn. Relics are excluded for a different reason: `player.relics` has no token fields, so a held relic cannot carry a secret at all yet (a gap `effects.ts` has documented since unit 3). | Law §4.3.1, §4.3.2, §7.1.1, §7.1.2 |
